You · never posted

One side says men are useless. The other says real men shouldn't have to. Neither one is talking about you. So you keep your head down, keep going, and when anyone asks, you say the same two words.

"I'm good."

At work.

Translation: I cleared my inbox before the kids woke up, covered for someone in the 2:00, and I'll be back on after bedtime. If I say that out loud, I look like I can't handle it.

At home.

Translation: the field trip form is signed, the dentist is rebooked, and the baby was up at 3. She had a worse day than I did, so somebody in this house has to be good. Tonight it's me.

Online.

Translation: I'm staying out of this. Anything I say about my side will get me dragged.

I was "good" right up until I fell asleep at the wheel.

The crash woke me up, and I believe God did too. I'd spent the day pouring into other people's kids and had nothing left for the drive home to my own. I walked away from it. A lot of us won't get that warning.

So I stopped running on "good." I'm building the system I wish I'd had: something that carries the mental load before it carries me off the road, and a crew of men I can actually tell the truth to. That's Run Home.
